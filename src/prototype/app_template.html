  Research report · v1.0 · 11 October 2026
  
Capital in Motion: elections, institutional stability and international capital flows, 2015–2026

  
SummaryQuestionDataMethodResultsHeterogeneityDynamicsRobustnessPost-hocLimitationsConclusion


  
Summary

  
We test whether national elections, institutional quality and global financial conditions change cross-border capital flows in 108 economies (quarterly) and 121 economies (annual) between 2015 and 2025. Flows are IMF balance-of-payments data under the BPM6 manual. Gross inflows are new liabilities to non-residents; gross outflows are new foreign assets acquired by residents. Election dates are triangulated across Wikipedia's national electoral calendars, International IDEA and V-Dem.

  
Pre-registered result. Gross inflows and outflows do not change in the two-quarter window covering the election quarter and the quarter before it. Institutions, development level and asset class do not change that conclusion. All four primary tests have Holm-adjusted p = 1.00.

  
Two patterns sit outside the confirmatory tests. After elections, gross inflows and outflows fall together by about 1.4 pp of GDP while net flows stay flat. That pattern is directionally stable but statistically fragile. When the window is narrowed to the election quarter alone, matching the closest prior IMF study, inflows to emerging and developing economies fall by about 2.15 pp of GDP, roughly a third of their average level. That result passes a placebo test and seasonality controls, but it was specified after the main results were known.


  
Question and hypotheses

  
The push–pull framework explains capital flows by global factors and domestic fundamentals, and rarely includes dated political events. Firms delay irreversible investment around elections (Julio & Yook, 2012), US multinationals' FDI to foreign affiliates dips before host-country elections (Julio & Yook, 2016), and an IMF working paper finds gross private inflows to 38 emerging markets fall around elections, 1990–2020 (Arakelyan & Evdokimova, 2025). We asked whether this holds in a broader and more recent sample.

  
    
    
  • H1a / H1b: gross inflows / outflows differ in the pre-election window.
    • 
    
  • H2: the association varies with rule of law (fixed at 2014).
    • 
    
  • H3: responses differ across FDI, portfolio equity, portfolio debt and other investment.
    • 
    
  • H5: exposure to global risk (VIX) varies with rule of law, reserve cover and short-term debt.
    • 
    
  • H6: the association differs between advanced and emerging/developing economies.
    • 
  


  
Data

  
The country universe follows a fixed rule: World Bank-listed, population of at least 1 million in 2014, complete gross flows for at least 9 of 11 years, and a 2014 governance score. Of 196 candidates, 121 qualify annually (33 advanced, 88 emerging/developing) and 108 quarterly (33 and 75). No country was added or removed by hand.

  
Election dates needed three sources. International IDEA records about half of recent elections with the year only, so Wikipedia's national electoral calendars supply exact dates and each contest must be confirmed by IDEA or V-Dem to enter the analysis. That leaves 282 executive-relevant elections in 2015–2025: 195 scheduled, 71 early by our rule, 16 unclassified. Validation checks pass: the BPM6 financial-account identity holds within 0.5% of GDP in every country-period, and a full rerun from the archived raw data reproduces every table byte for byte.

Method

  
The baseline regresses a flow outcome on a pre-election window (the election quarter and the one before) and a post-election window (the two quarters after), with country and quarter fixed effects and the previous year's macroeconomic fundamentals. Controls are lagged so the model does not condition on channels through which elections act, such as exchange-rate moves. Standard errors are clustered by country, and Driscoll–Kraay, two-way clustered and wild-bootstrap inference are reported alongside. Causal language is reserved for scheduled elections, whose timing is fixed years in advance.


  
Main results

  

  
The confidence interval for H1a rules out pre-election effects larger than about 1.6 pp of GDP, about 0.12 of a within-country standard deviation of quarterly inflows. Inference agrees across methods: country-clustered SE 0.73, Driscoll–Kraay 0.45, two-way clustered 0.68.


  
Heterogeneity

  
None of the eight institutional measures, from WGI rule of law to V-Dem judicial constraints, significantly changes the pre-election association. In emerging and developing economies alone the pre-window estimate is −0.90 (p = 0.08), against +0.77 in advanced economies, but the difference is not significant (p = 0.39). Components do not differ (joint test p = 0.43). A higher VIX lowers inflows more where 2014 reserve cover was higher (−0.50 pp per standard deviation, p = 0.09), but nothing in the global-risk family survives adjustment. Sudden stops do not predict unrest, early elections or a change of chief executive.


  
Dynamics

  
Gross inflows around scheduled executive-relevant elections, all economies
Reference quarter k = −4. 95% intervals clustered by country.

  
No quarter in the run-up is distinguishable from zero. Inflows dip in the first two quarters after scheduled elections and rebound in the third, which looks like a shift in timing more than a lasting change. A stacked design with clean comparison countries gives mostly negative post-election estimates but with standard errors of 3–5 pp, too wide to settle the question.


  
Robustness

  
Across 34 pre-specified tests the pre-election coefficient stays between −0.94 and +0.27 and is never significant at 5%. Dropping any single country moves it between −0.47 and +0.19, and placebo calendars give p = 0.84. The post-election coefficient is negative in every test, between −0.86 and −3.00, but p ranges from 0.014 to 0.27. It loses significance without financial centres, without 2020, and in the balanced panel.

Our pre-registered window merged the election quarter with the quarter before, which is slightly positive, so the run-up null and this effect are consistent with each other and with the IMF finding of no run-up decline. What does not replicate is the IMF's headline moderation: low political stability does not deepen the election-quarter decline here (interaction +0.08, p = 0.95). The EMDE result survives a Bonferroni correction for the six post-hoc models, 1,000 placebo calendars and country-by-season fixed effects. It should still be confirmed on new elections with the window fixed in advance.


  
Limitations

  
    
    
  • Quarterly flows are very noisy (within-country SD of 10–20 pp of GDP annualised), so effects smaller than about 0.1 SD cannot be ruled out.
    • 
    
  • The executive-relevance and early-election rules are approximations.
    • 
    
  • Not coded: election closeness, coalition changes, government-formation delays.
    • 
    
  • Only the revised 2026 vintage of the governance indicators is available.
    • 
    
  • Not used: UNCTAD greenfield and M&A data, and BIS banking statistics.
    • 
    
  • Policy events are derived from data rather than hand-coded, so policy results are exploratory.
    • 
  


  
Conclusion

  
In 2015–2025, capital flows do not move in the run-up to elections across this broad sample. The evidence points instead to a decline in emerging-economy inflows at the moment of the vote and a short-lived gross retrenchment afterwards. The institutional cushioning reported before does not appear. These are statements about association and about scheduled election timing under stated assumptions. They do not rank countries or political systems.

Glossary

  
How the country analysis works

    
Driver model. For each country group (advanced; emerging and developing), quarterly flows in 2015Q1–2025Q4 are regressed on last quarter's flow (momentum), global push factors (VIX, US policy rate, change in the US 10-year yield, dollar and oil price changes), last year's domestic fundamentals from the IMF WEO (growth, inflation, current account, fiscal balance, public debt), the change in commodity terms of trade (IMF CTOT) and the real exchange rate (IMF EER), the real interest-rate gap with the US (World Bank; 49% coverage, gaps set to the country average) and the election-quarter and post-election windows, with country fixed effects. Each country gets its own sensitivity to the global factors, shrunk toward the group estimate (empirical Bayes) because 44 quarters is too few to estimate them alone.

    
Why. With country fixed effects, each quarter's deviation from the country's own average splits exactly into driver contributions plus an unexplained remainder. These are statistical attributions, not causal effects. The model explains only a small share of quarterly movement, which is typical for gross capital flows.

    
Outlook. The central forecast blends the driver model, the average of the last four reported quarters and the 2015–25 average. Weights are chosen per group and flow by minimising error in a 2024–25 backtest (estimated to 2023Q4). For gross flows the backtest gave the driver model no weight, so the central path rests on the recent level and the long-run average, plus the election effect where that improved accuracy. Ranges come from backtest errors at each horizon, scaled by the country's volatility; 80% ranges contained 79–84% of 2024–25 outcomes. Scenario sliders apply the model's country sensitivities on top of the central path.

    
Risk. Flows at risk (gross and net) come from quantile regressions blended with each country's history, with tails recalibrated from past errors. Sudden-stop probabilities give the chance that a new episode starts, under a gross and a net definition. The early-warning score uses thresholds chosen by the signals method. Methods were chosen on forecasts made in 2019–22 and scored on 2023–24 (Track record tab). Every live forecast is stored in a ledger so it can be scored later.

Sources

  

    
Definitions

      
Gross inflows: FDI + portfolio + other investment, net incurrence of liabilities (D_F, P_F, O_F · L_NIL_T).

      
Gross outflows: the same categories, net acquisition of assets by residents (A_NFA_T), reserves excluded.

      
Net flows: inflows − outflows; positive means a net inflow.

      
Private inflows (post-hoc): gross inflows minus central-bank and general-government other-investment liabilities and SDR allocations.

      
Scaling: quarterly flow × 4 ÷ annual GDP × 100, winsorised at the 1st/99th percentiles in estimation.

    
Election coding

      
Wikipedia national electoral calendars give dates. A contest enters only if International IDEA or V-Dem also records it in that year.

      
Executive-relevant: the presidential contest where V-Dem codes the chief executive as directly elected, otherwise the lower-house contest.

      
Early: more than 6 months earlier than the country's typical 1990–2014 term implies.

      
Windows: pre = election quarter and the quarter before; post = the next two quarters.
